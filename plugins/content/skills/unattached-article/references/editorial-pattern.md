# Editorial composition pattern

## Opening

Answer three questions immediately: what is being investigated, what evidence
is most surprising, and why should the reader continue?

Use this sequence:

`issue frame → title and concise lede → hierarchical evidence band → thesis or
documentary plate → executive summary → explanatory overview → TOC`

Make the evidence band a miniature argument such as `concentration → mechanism
→ dependence → consequence`. Make one figure dominant. Never headline counts
of chapters, sources, mechanisms, or research dimensions.

## Narrative

Rewrite from evidence. Do not convert source headings directly into chapters.
Build 6-12 chapters that move from a concrete scene or contradiction to the
system behind it, test counterexamples, then end with the implication. Before
drafting prose, state privately what each chapter proves.

## Visual coverage

Cover at least 80% of substantive chapters:

- Person or institution: licensed portrait, headquarters, artifact, or event.
- Ownership: proportional bar chart or control-chain diagram.
- Money: dated comparison chart with units and source note.
- History: exact event photograph, archival page, or timeline.
- Process: labeled flow diagram showing actors and consequences.
- Conclusion: multi-lever system map.

Alternate full-width photography, compact charts, comparisons, timelines, and
relationship maps. A caption should identify the subject, explain why it
matters, then give creator and license.

## Asset budget

Prefer publication-size derivatives. Otherwise resize photographs to at most
1200 px on the long edge, strip metadata, and encode near JPEG quality 76.
Preserve PNG only for transparency or line art. Compare the built artifact with
recent flagship pages before deployment.
